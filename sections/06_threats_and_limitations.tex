\subsubsection{Key Empirical Insights on High-Capacity Regimes}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Learned Routers Fluctuate Around Static PoE:} In this high-capacity regime, Static 50/50 PoE achieves $772.2 \pm 17.3$ hits. The Scalar Router attains $775.6 \pm 19.3$ hits ($\Delta = +3.4$), the 5D Router attains $769.0 \pm 28.5$ hits ($\Delta = -3.2$), candidate-level Learning-to-Rank (LTR) achieves $775.0 \pm 18.6$ hits ($\Delta = +2.8$), and CC-PoE achieves $772.9 \pm 18.7$ hits ($\Delta = +0.7$). Paired McNemar tests against Static PoE confirm that none of these router differences reach statistical significance ($p > 0.25$ across all evaluated pairs). Rather than strictly collapsing to static weights, adaptive architectures fluctuate within the sampling noise floor of stationary fusion.
    \item \textbf{Statistical Power \& Equivalence Testing (TOST):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Minimal Detectable Effect (MDE):} With $N=6{,}040$ and between $50$ and $104$ discordant pairs per seed, our paired tests possess $80\%$ statistical power ($\alpha = 0.05$) to detect true performance shifts of $\text{MDE} \in [19.8, 28.6]$ hits ($\Delta \text{HR@10} \in [0.33\%, 0.47\%]$). Consequently, we cannot rule out smaller latent deltas in the range of $+5$ to $+18$ hits.
        \item \emph{Two One-Sided Tests (TOST):} Under formal equivalence testing against Static PoE with an \emph{a priori} equivalence margin of $\pm 30$ hits ($\pm 0.50\%$ in $\text{HR@10}$), the null hypothesis of non-equivalence is rejected across all 5 seeds ($p_{\text{TOST}} \le 0.011$; representative Seed 123 $90\%$ CI: $[-7.3, +11.3]$ hits). However, seed-level heterogeneity is real: on Seed 2024, the $90\%$ CI is $[+4.2, +25.8]$ hits, indicating a localized positive shift, while other seeds sit tightly around zero. We therefore conclude that while large routing advantages ($>30$ hits) can be statistically excluded, learned routers exhibit coarse practical indifference to static fusion rather than exact mathematical identity.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Backbone Retraining Variance Dominates Fusion Delta:} Across seeds, backbone training stochasticity ($733 \to 804$ hits, $\pm 35.4$ hits) is substantially larger than the average gain of static calibration ($+7.8$ hits).
    \item \textbf{The Fixed Disagreement Penalty Does Not Transfer:} The fixed penalty ($\lambda=0.21$), which yielded consistent gains in the compact regime, degrades performance on 4 out of 5 backbone pairs (mean $\Delta = -17.2$, negative on seeds 123, 2024, 7, and 99). Candidate disagreement filtering is sensitive to relative expert competence and cannot be transferred without retraining.
    \item \textbf{Bounded Oracle Headroom and Asymmetric Risk:} The discrete Oracle headroom narrows from $+45.2\%$ in the compact regime to $+7.96\%$ ($+60.6$ hits over SASRec alone). Hit-overlap contingency analysis reveals why routing is difficult: across the 5 pairs, the mean number of isolated semantic hits ($n_{01}$, where Semantic succeeds but SASRec fails) is merely $42.0 \pm 18.2$, whereas the number of isolated collaborative hits ($n_{10}$, where SASRec succeeds but Semantic fails) is $201.6 \pm 32.8$. A router attempting to capture the $\approx 42$ semantic hits risks demoting the $\approx 201$ collaborative hits.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Threats to Validity \& Methodological Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{History Masking Evaluation:} In top-$K$ recommendation, masking seen items during full-catalog evaluation is common. Auditing on MovieLens-1M shows that history masking alters performance marginally (SASRec: $0.0515 \to 0.0508$; Static PoE: $0.0725 \to 0.0714$; Fixed Disagreement PoE: $0.0745 \to 0.0730$). Under history masking, Fixed Disagreement PoE preserves $+10$ net hits over Static PoE ($441$ vs. $431$).
    \item \textbf{Population Sizing ($N=6{,}038$ vs. $N=6{,}040$):} Raw MovieLens-1M contains $6{,}040$ users. The primary compact benchmark filters 2 users with history length $< 5$, yielding exactly $N = 6{,}038$ users. The high-capacity backbone retraining pipeline retains all $N = 6{,}040$ users ($n_{00} + n_{01} + n_{10} + n_{11} = 6{,}040$), accounting for the 2-instance difference across protocols.
    \item \textbf{Semantic Backbone Choice:} Our semantic expert utilizes frozen MiniLM representations ($0.0449$). While modern LLM-based recommenders report stronger absolute scores, the paired SASRec + MiniLM setup provides a clean, controlled environment to study the geometric mechanics of dual-expert routing without multi-billion-parameter confounding factors.
\end{enumerate}
