\subsubsection{Mathematical Equivalence of Oracle-RR and True Max-Hit Upper Bound}
We formalize that selecting the action maximizing reciprocal rank ($a^*_{\text{RR}}(u) \triangleq \arg\max_{a \in \{C, S, H\}} \text{RR}_a(u)$) is mathematically and empirically identical to the combinatorial Max-Hit upper bound:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{1}\left[\text{rank}_{a^*_{\text{RR}}}(u) \le K\right] \equiv \max_{a \in \{C, S, H\}} \mathbf{1}\left[\text{rank}_a(u) \le K\right]
\end{equation}
Because $\text{rank}_{a^*_{\text{RR}}}(u) = \min_{a} \text{rank}_a(u)$, the minimum of integer ranks is $\le K$ if and only if at least one action achieves a rank $\le K$. Across all $6{,}038$ test sequences, \textbf{exactly zero mismatches exist between Oracle-RR and Max-Hit@10}. Hence, the reported Oracle HR@10 of $0.0931$ is the exact upper bound achievable by any action selector over $\mathcal{A} = \{C, S, H\}$.

\subsection{Statistical Signatures of Minority Specialists (RQ3)}
\label{sec:signatures}

We conduct two-sample Welch's $t$-tests across the candidate features with Bonferroni correction ($\alpha_{\text{Bonf}} = 0.05/12 = 4.17 \times 10^{-3}$) and Glass's $\Delta$ using Cohort $H$ as reference scale:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Collaborative Entropy $H_c$:} Strongly suppressed in both $C$ ($\Delta = -0.364, p < 10^{-11}$) and $S$ ($\Delta = -0.557, p < 10^{-19}$). Thus, $H_c$ serves as a \textbf{Universal Non-Consensus Detector}.
    \item \textbf{Collaborative Margin $M_c$:} Slightly elevated in $C$ ($p_{\text{Bonf}} = 0.0410, \Delta = +0.182$), acting as the \textbf{sole detectable directional signal}.
    \item \textbf{Absence of Direct $C$ vs. $S$ Separation:} \textbf{Not a single feature achieves statistically significant separation directly between $C$ and $S$ after multiple testing correction ($p_{\text{Bonf}} > 0.05$ across all tests)}.
\end{itemize}
This establishes the \emph{Shared-Signal Dilemma}: features reliably detect when an instance departs from consensus, but cannot identify which expert to trust.

\subsection{Causal Analysis \& Information-Theoretic Bound via Fano's Inequality}
\label{sec:fano}

Consider the Structural Causal Model (SCM):
\begin{equation}
    U \longrightarrow \mathcal{S}_u \longrightarrow \mathbf{x}_u \longrightarrow \hat{W}_u \longrightarrow \hat{S}(u, \cdot), \qquad \mathcal{S}_u \longrightarrow V_t^* \longrightarrow Y^* \in \{C, S, H\}
\end{equation}
Because sequence summary vector $\mathbf{x}_u = \phi(\mathcal{S}_u)$ is computed strictly over historical items, the target item $V_t^*$ is conditionally independent: $V_t^* \perp \mathbf{x}_u \mid \mathcal{S}_u$.
Using the non-parametric KSG estimator~\cite{kraskov2004estimating}, we estimate:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{I}(Y^* ; \mathbf{x}_u \mid \text{Top-10 Consensus}) \approx 0.041 \text{ nats} \approx 0.059 \text{ bits}
\end{equation}
Applying Fano's Inequality~\cite{fano1961transmission} with reference prior $H(Y^*) \approx \log_2 3 = 1.585$ bits yields an indicative classification error lower bound:
\begin{equation}
    H_b(P_e) + P_e \ge 1.585 - 0.059 = 1.526 \implies P_e \ge \mathbf{0.53} \; (53\%)
\end{equation}
Empirically, training a neural classifier directly supervised on Oracle actions yields an error of \textbf{$54.75\%$}, leading to $\text{HR@10} = 0.0624$ (worse than static 50/50 at $0.0641$).

\subsubsection{Rich 128-d Latent Sequence Separability}
To evaluate whether this bottleneck is an artifact of low-dimensional scalar summaries, we extracted the full concatenated $128$-dimensional user representations $[\mathbf{h}_u^c, \mathbf{h}_u^s] \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ from the dual Transformer backbones:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Raw 128-d Latent + Regularized Logistic Regression:} $\text{ROC-AUC} = \mathbf{0.5828 \pm 0.0482}$.
    \item \textbf{PCA Dimensionality Reduction:} Yields $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.5767 \pm 0.0484$ (8 comps), $0.5765 \pm 0.0564$ (16 comps), and $0.5894 \pm 0.0532$ (32 comps).
    \item \textbf{L1-Regularized Sparse Classifier:} Degrades to $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.4693 \pm 0.0286$.
\end{itemize}
Even rich 128-d sequence representations fail to provide directional specialist foresight, confirming an intrinsic informational representation bottleneck.

\subsection{Prefix-Truncation Sensitivity: When Does Routing Win?}
\label{sec:prefix}

Evaluating across truncated prefix lengths ($L \in \{1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20\}$) reconciles global collapse with localized regime utility:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{At $L=1$ (Popularity Collapse):} SASRec achieves apparent $\text{HR@10} = 0.1327$ by recommending the identical 10 blockbuster items to 100\% of users (0.50\% catalog coverage, 0.9970 Gini concentration). The Oracle identifies Semantic as optimal for $92.9\%$ of instances.
    \item \textbf{At $L=5$ (Personalization Discovery):} The selective router achieves $\text{HR@10} = \mathbf{0.1413}$, decisively outperforming Static Fusion ($0.1227, p=0.0002$) and SASRec ($0.1260, p=0.0065$). Here, expert uncertainty asymmetry is pronounced and informative.
    \item \textbf{At $L=20$ (Collaborative Saturation):} SASRec degrades to $0.0413$ due to recency saturation; the router maintains $0.0667$ (\textbf{+61.5\%} gain, $p=0.0001$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comprehensive Component Ablation Across Authenticated Benchmarks}
\label{sec:cross_dataset_ablation}

To rigorously quantify the individual contribution of each architectural innovation within BiX-GDR, we perform an ablation study across all authenticated benchmark datasets. We evaluate four incremental variants under identical optimization protocols:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Base Dual-Stream (Static PoE):} Independent collaborative and semantic Transformers without intermediate cross-modal interaction, fused via stationary candidate logit addition ($S_c + S_s$).
    \item \textbf{+ Bilateral Cross-Attention (BiX-Vanilla):} Introduces intermediate layer-wise co-attention ($C \leftrightarrow S$) with standard residual addition ($h + C$), without adaptive gating or candidate regularization.
    \item \textbf{+ Adaptive Information Injection Gates (BiX-Gate):} Equips cross-attention residuals with dynamic sigmoid gates $\mathbf{g} = \sigma(\mathbf{W}[h \,\|\, C])$ to regulate cross-modal flow and suppress noise.
    \item \textbf{+ Geometric Discrepancy Regularization (BiX-GDR Full):} Full architecture combining gated bilateral cross-attention with candidate-level divergence penalization ($-\lambda |S_c - S_s|$).
\end{enumerate}

The ablation trajectory in Table~\ref{tab:cross_dataset_ablation} reveals several fundamental structural insights:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Bilateral Cross-Attention is the Primary Performance Engine:} Moving cross-modal interaction from post-hoc score blending to intermediate Transformer representations accounts for $>85\%$ of the total net gain on primary benchmarks ($+68.8$ hits on MovieLens-1M and $+66.0$ hits on Amazon Beauty). This demonstrates that contextualizing behavioral transitions with semantic manifold geometry prior to candidate scoring is the decisive remedy for late routing stagnation.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Information Gating Shields Sparse Trajectories from Noise:} On ultra-sparse catalogs (Amazon Beauty), unconstrained residual addition allows semantic representations to dilute sparse sequential signals. The adaptive gate $\mathbf{g}$ dynamically throttles cross-modal injection on low-confidence transitions, recovering $+6.7$ hits on Amazon Beauty ($366.3 \to 373.0$ hits).
    \item \textbf{Geometric Discrepancy Regularization Secures Peak Precision:} Applying candidate-level geometric discrepancy penalization ($-\lambda |S_c - S_s|$) provides an additional consistent gain ($+2.6$ hits on MovieLens-1M, $+4.0$ hits on Amazon Beauty). By explicitly penalizing divergent candidate rankings, it eliminates false-positive tail intrusions and maximizes precision across all ecosystems.
\end{itemize}
