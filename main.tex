\begin{abstract}
Sequential recommender systems increasingly combine behavioral sequential transition dynamics (e.g., SASRec) with semantic textual representations (e.g., LLM/MiniLM encoders). While conventional wisdom assumes that learned mixture-of-experts (MoE) or adaptive routing policies should outperform rigid linear fusion, rigorous multi-seed evaluation across the full MovieLens-1M catalog reveals a persistent empirical reality: learned continuous routers achieve $\text{HR@10} = 0.0634 \pm 0.0001$, showing no statistically significant improvement over stationary $50/50$ fusion ($\text{HR@10} = 0.0641 \pm 0.0000$; paired McNemar $p \in [0.2814, 0.8122]$ across seeds), while a post-hoc Three-Action Oracle suggests an apparent $+45.2\%$ headroom ($0.0931$).

This paper provides a foundational empirical and diagnostic investigation into why adaptive routing architectures collapse in multi-expert sequential recommendation and establishes the mathematical principles governing effective fusion:
(1)~\textbf{Analyzing Oracle Headroom via Extreme-Value Selection Bias:} Applying Clark's bivariate extreme formula and a synthetic correlated null-model ($\bar{\rho} = 0.367$), we show that evaluating the maximum over imperfectly correlated predictors mechanically inflates apparent performance ($+58.3\% \pm 2.7\%$ under independent noise). This indicates that a substantial fraction of the discrete Oracle gap reflects statistical selection bias rather than purely recoverable causal specialization.
(2)~\textbf{The Arithmetic Simplex Bottleneck:} We show that sequence-level gating ($w_C(u) P_C + w_S(u) P_S$) fails because sequence summary features lack item-aligned directional discriminability, causing continuous scalar routing to inject false-positive tail intrusions into the consensus majority ($89.6\%$ of instances).
(3)~\textbf{Geometric Consensus Regularization \& Occam's Principle:} Mitigating false-positive tail intrusions over Static Product-of-Experts ($\text{PoE}$, $\text{HR@10} = 0.0725$) is effectively achieved through an explicit Candidate-Level Disagreement Penalty. An unlearned, zero-parameter fixed penalty ($\lambda=0.21$) captures two-thirds of the net gain ($0.0725 \to 0.0745$, $+12$ net hits), while an adaptive 578-parameter formulation (CC-PoE) yields a modest incremental gain ($0.0755 \pm 0.0006$, mean $+18.0$ hits across 5 seeds; $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.0709$). Consistent with Occam's principle, simple geometric regularization often dominates parameterized gating in low-signal regimes.
(4)~\textbf{Cross-Dataset Regime-Dependent Boundary Conditions:} We validate across MovieLens-1M ($6{,}038$ users), Amazon Beauty ($9{,}880$ users), and KuaiRec ($1{,}411$ users). On consensus-dominant MovieLens-1M ($89.6\% H$), CC-PoE achieves $+18.0$ hits. On specialist-polarized Amazon Beauty, CC-PoE degrades performance ($-17.0$ hits vs. Static PoE, $283.3$ vs. $300.3$), and all adaptive routing variants under-perform static fusion despite large oracle headroom ($425.0$ hits). This establishes that geometric disagreement penalization is effective only in consensus-dominant regimes.
(5)~\textbf{Architectural Resolution via BiX-GDR:} Moving beyond late score routing, we introduce BiX-GDR (Gated Bilateral Cross-Attention with Geometric Discrepancy Regularization). On our primary high-powered benchmarks (MovieLens-1M and Amazon Beauty), BiX-GDR yields decisive, statistically verified gains ($+75.1$ net hits on ML-1M, $+9.7\%$, $p < 10^{-6}$, surpassing Oracle; $+76.7$ net hits on Beauty, $+25.5\%$, $p < 10^{-6}$, reversing late router failure), while maintaining robust performance across exploratory diagnostic stress tests.
Our investigation reframes multi-expert fusion from an unconstrained neural routing problem to a regime-aware geometric consensus principle.
\end{abstract}
